# Changelog

## Unreleased

### Added

- TIFF files of more kinds are read: BigTIFF (offsets of 8 bytes, in either byte order; its colour profile, XMP packet, resolution and EXIF block read as a classic TIFF's, so `AutoOrient()` turns it), greys and palettes of 2 and 4 bits, grey with alpha (8 and 16 bits, multiplied by the alpha or not, black or white as zero), and CMYK (8 bits, and 16 bits reduced to 8), converted to sRGB through the file's profile or, without one, through the CMYK profile a CMYK JPEG without one goes through. Checked: the greys and palettes against Pillow (the same pixels), the CMYK conversion against LittleCMS (within 1 of 255), and thirteen kinds of BigTIFF that libvips writes (strips, tiles, a pyramid, every compression, 16 bits, CMYK, alpha, a palette) against the classic file of the same content (the same pixels).
- TIFF, further: blocks compressed with zstd (compression 50000, as libtiff numbers it; the Go standard library's decoder, copied to `internal/zstd`, with a window no larger than a block and never above 8MB) and blocks that are WebP images (50001; lossy or lossless, RGB with or without alpha, to the pixels libvips reads); floating point samples (16, 32 and 64 bits; grey, RGB, RGB with alpha; the horizontal predictor and libtiff's floating point predictor) taken as linear light from 0 to 1, as libvips reads them and as PFM is read here, and made sRGB of 16 bits (four kinds written by libvips come out as libvips reads them, to the pixel); CIELAB (8 and 16 bits and floats, and with the offsets of ICC) made sRGB through D50, the white libtiff and Photoshop give a Lab TIFF, within half a level of the formulas (LittleCMS, which interpolates in a table, is up to 15 of 255 from them; libvips' own Lab files, whose Lab is relative to D65, come out up to 17 of 255 from what libvips reads). The libvips 8.18.7 and Pillow 12.1.1 builds for Windows read no zstd TIFF, and Pillow no float RGB or float Lab.
- IPTC: `KeepIptc()` (`-keep-iptc`) copies the IPTC record of the input (the press's metadata: caption, keywords, byline, copyright notice) to JPEG and TIFF output; `Info.HasIptc`, `inspect.ReadIptc` (the record's datasets, as sharp's `metadata().iptc` gives a TIFF's), `EncodeOptions.Iptc` and `pipeline.Meta.Iptc` for formats of one's own; `goimgtools info` names it. Read from JPEG (Photoshop's resource 0x0404 in APP13 segments, in one or split over several), TIFF (tag 33723, of bytes or of LONGs as Photoshop writes it, also when wrapped in Photoshop's resources) and Photoshop files; written to JPEG as Photoshop writes it (sharp reads the same bytes from the output as from the input) and to TIFF as the record itself. Dropped by default, as EXIF and XMP are. (libvips 8.18.7, copying a JPEG's record to a TIFF, keeps a quarter of it: 32 of 128 bytes in the file checked.)
- Six more formats are read (none of them written), each a band of rows at a time within a few hundred kilobytes whatever the size of the image: **PCX** (`.pcx`: 1 to 8 bits a plane, 1 to 4 planes, run-length coded or not, the palette of the header or the one of 256 colours at the end; a header palette left all black is taken as the EGA colours), **SGI** (`.sgi`, `.rgb`, `.rgba`, `.bw`: 1 to 4 channels of 8 or 16 bits, stored or run-length coded, its rows read from the bottom up where they lie), **XBM** (`.xbm`: a set bit is black, as X11 draws it and ffmpeg writes it; Pillow reads and writes the opposite), **XPM** version 3 (`.xpm`: 1 to 4 characters a pixel, up to 65,536 colours, `None` transparent, colours as X11 reads them: `#rgb` digits are the high ones, its colour names), **DDS** (`.dds`, the top image: uncompressed with any channel masks, DXT1/3/5, ATI1/2, and the DX10 header with BC1 to BC5 and BC7; to Pillow's pixels on 50 files) and **FITS** (`.fits`, `.fit`, `.fts`: the image of the primary unit or of the first image extension, 8 to 64-bit integers and 32 and 64-bit floats, with BZERO and BSCALE, big-endian as the standard has it — Pillow 12.1.1 reads the 16 and 32-bit ones with their bytes swapped and ignores BZERO — to 8 bits from 8 and to 16 bits clamped otherwise). Checked against Pillow and ffmpeg file by file (see each package's tests); damaged files are an error, never more memory than the limit.
- `ColorLUT(cube)` (`-lut file.cube`), as Pillow's `Image.filter` with a `Color3DLUT`, to the byte: every pixel gets the colour a lookup cube has for its colour, interpolated between the eight points around it. A cube (`LUT3D`) is read from a `.cube` file of Adobe's or DaVinci Resolve's kind by `ParseCube` (2 to 65 points a side, `DOMAIN_MIN` and `DOMAIN_MAX` honoured) or made from a function by `NewLUT3D` (Pillow's `Color3DLUT.generate`). Checked on seven cubes against Pillow 12.1.1, among them values beyond 0 to 1 and below 0.
- `Draw` takes three more shapes, as Pillow's ImageDraw has them: `Arc(cx, cy, rx, ry, start, end)` (a line along the ellipse), `Pie(...)` (the slice to the centre) and `Chord(...)` (closed by a straight line), the angles in degrees clockwise from three o'clock; by name `arc`, `pie` and `chord` for `Do` and `-op`.
- `Noise(width, height, NoiseOptions{Mean, Sigma, Seed})`: a grey image of Gaussian noise, as Pillow's `Image.effect_noise` and libvips' `gaussnoise` make one (the sum of twelve random numbers, as libvips), the same for a seed on every processor and however it is cut into strips; made as it is read, without memory of its size.
- `FloodFill(x, y, colour, FloodFillOptions{Threshold, Border})`, as Pillow's `ImageDraw.floodfill`, to the pixel: the area of one colour around a point (the pixels joined to it side by side, of its colour or within a threshold of it; or, with a border colour, everything up to that colour) is painted. The image is kept in working storage (a temporary file beyond its share of the memory limit) and two bits a pixel in memory, 6MB for 24 megapixels; the area is found by sweeping its rows down and up until nothing changes, so its shape costs time, not memory. Checked on 16 cases against Pillow 12.1.1 (thresholds, a border, a spiral corridor, pictures with transparency).
- [Comparison with other tools](comparison.md): every capability against the strongest tool for it (libvips 8.18.7, sharp 0.35.5, Pillow 12.1.1, cwebp, mozjpeg, jpegli, gifsicle, LittleCMS, librsvg), the same task and input, time and peak memory of the whole process, quality by SSIMULACRA2 and butteraugli, the formats and features of each, and the list of what is behind; the scripts that measure it in `tests/compare`.
- SVG text draws colour glyphs of COLR version 1 (as Noto Color Emoji is made, and as Chrome draws them): their paint graphs of glyph outlines that clip, solid colours, linear, radial and sweep gradients (their colours interpolated premultiplied; the stops beyond 0 to 1 and the pad, repeat and reflect extends), transforms (translate, scale, rotate, skew, about a centre), clip boxes and composites (the blend modes; of Porter and Duff's, clear, source and destination, the others as source over), the text's fill as the foreground colour. A font with such glyphs is taken for emoji as a colour font. Variations of such fonts are not applied. Text drawn by `Text()` still draws them in one colour.
- SVG text in fonts with variations (an `fvar` table: TrueType outlines with `gvar`, or CFF2) is drawn at the instance of its style, as Chrome sets it: `font-weight` on the `wght` axis, `font-stretch` (keywords and percentages, now read) on `wdth`, `font-style: oblique` (and its angle) on `slnt`, `italic` on `ital`, the font size on `opsz` (optical sizing, CSS's default), and `font-variation-settings` over them; outlines, advances and shaping all follow (it was drawn at the font's default instance, so a bold of a family whose only font is variable came out regular). The outlines and their variations are read for such fonts only, counted in the memory limit.
- SVG vertical text: `writing-mode` `tb`, `tb-rl`, `vertical-rl` and `vertical-lr` on a text element (inherited, as Chrome takes it; not on a tspan) set its line down from `y`, across `x` on its central baseline: letters of most scripts lie on their side; the Chinese, Japanese and Korean scripts, their punctuation, full-width forms and emoji stand upright in the middle of the line, each advancing and placed by the font's vertical metrics (`vhea` and `vmtx`, the top of the outline its top bearing below the pen, as HarfBuzz places it for Chrome; a font without them by its height and ascent), brackets and dashes on their side; `text-orientation` `upright` stands every glyph, `sideways` none. `x`, `y`, `dx`, `dy`, `text-anchor`, `rotate`, decorations and the box of the text follow the line. Text on a path stays horizontal.
- SVG text on paths as Chrome lays it out: SVG 2's `path` attribute of `textPath` (what of it parses, up to its first error, taken before `href`); an absolute `x` of a character on a path as its distance along the path (`x` on the text, or on a tspan inside the textPath, moves the text along it; it was passed over); `x` and `y` on the textPath itself, which are not its attributes, passed over (they were taken).
- SVG: the filter functions of CSS (`filter="blur(2px) drop-shadow(4px 5px 6px blue) grayscale(0.5)"`: blur, brightness, contrast, drop-shadow, grayscale, hue-rotate, invert, opacity, saturate, sepia, and url() among them), each through the primitives the Filter Effects specification gives it, in sRGB, one after the other, over the element's box with its strokes and as far around it as they reach (a url()'s filter cut to its own region), as Chrome draws them: drop-shadow's third length the blur's standard deviation, lengths without a unit taken as pixels; a list Chrome takes for none (a percentage in blur, an angle without a unit, commas between the arguments) leaves the element unfiltered. On resvg's tests of filter functions, 36 of 44 pass (were 15; the others differ only in their labels' font).
- SVG style sheets apply the rules of a media query that holds for a picture shown on a screen in the light colour scheme (`@media (prefers-color-scheme: light)`, `screen`, `not print`, with `and`, `not` and commas); a query on sizes or other features does not hold. The rules of every `@media` were left out (those for the dark scheme, as pictures made for GitHub carry, still are).
- SVG: the HTML of a `foreignObject` is drawn, as mermaid writes the labels of its diagrams (with no SVG text in their place): its text in the fonts, sizes, weights and colours its CSS gives (the `color` property, as HTML has it; the document's style sheets apply to its elements), its lines broken by `br`, paragraphs, headings and other blocks (their margins as browsers give them; `line-height: normal` from the fonts that draw the line, a fallback font's taller lines making it taller, as in Chrome), aligned by `text-align` (inherited from the SVG elements it is in), the backgrounds of its inline elements and blocks under it, an SVG document inside the HTML drawn as SVG, and what overflows its box clipped; elements browsers do not show (style, script, head) are not drawn; lines wider than the box wrap at spaces, after hyphens between letters and between the characters of Chinese, Japanese and Korean (not before closing punctuation), never within a word that inline elements split, where `white-space` lets them (mermaid's `break-spaces` for long labels). On seven mermaid diagrams taken from open-source projects, the labels stand where Chrome draws them (they were missing); a draw.io label still takes the SVG text its export puts beside it.
- SVG text takes the face of its family that CSS matches to its `font-weight`, as Chrome does: 600 the family's semibold face (Segoe UI Semibold), 300 its light one, 900 its black one, each looked for by the names fonts give their weights (Thin, ExtraLight, Light, Semilight, Medium, Semibold, Bold, ExtraBold, Black) in the order CSS tries them, and `bolder` and `lighter` reckoned from the parent's weight as CSS has them. Only regular and bold were told apart before (600 to 900 took the bold face, other weights the regular one).
- SVG, toward the test suite of resvg and what Chrome draws: documents in encodings other than UTF-8, as their XML declaration names them by the labels browsers know (the single-byte encodings of Windows, ISO 8859, KOI8 and the Mac, GBK and GB18030, Big5, Shift_JIS, EUC-JP, ISO-2022-JP, EUC-KR; iso-8859-1 and us-ascii read as windows-1252, as browsers read them; an unknown one as UTF-8), and in UTF-16 (none of these was read before); the text entities a doctype declares (as Illustrator writes them); `transform-origin` (keywords, lengths and percentages of the viewport, as an attribute or in CSS; on elements, clip paths, gradients and patterns), the `transform` property of CSS (its functions and units, `none`; over the attribute) and the root's `transform` (about its centre, as Chrome); `context-fill` and `context-stroke` (SVG 2) in markers and in what a `use` element draws; `paint-order` placing the markers too (a value naming a part twice or an unknown word is not one); the CSS background colour of the root (`background-color`, `background`, as mermaid and draw.io write it), under the drawing as browsers paint it; SVG pictures in `image` elements (data URIs, compressed by gzip too), drawn at the size they take on the device, up to four deep, the pixels of the pictures of them all counted together; attribute selectors in style sheets (`[x]`, `=`, `~=`, `|=`, `^=`, `$=`, `*=`, ` i`); style elements of a type other than CSS left out; comments in `style` attributes; a marker's `orient` in `rad`, `grad` and `turn`; `inherit` for `stop-color`, `stop-opacity` and `opacity`; `color-interpolation: linearRGB` on gradients. Fixed with them: a gradient's stops are styled where the gradient is (their `currentColor` was the painted element's); a radial gradient referring to a linear one, or the reverse, takes its stops, units, transform and spread but not its geometry; a `gradientTransform` or `patternTransform` that does not parse gives way to that of the gradient it refers to, and one that flattens everything is left out; attributes of other namespaces (Inkscape's `sodipodi:cx`, any declared prefix) no longer stand for the attribute of the same name; the opacity of a `use` element of a `symbol` or `svg` element is applied, and an `svg` element drawn by `use` keeps its `x` and `y`; what `display: none` hides takes no room in bounding boxes, and a root with it draws nothing; a gradient without stops is painted with the fallback colour after it. On resvg's test suite (rendered at the size of its references; a test passes within a mean difference of 2), 1,223 of 1,801 tests pass (were 1,134); leaving out its text tests, which use fonts it ships, 1,202 of 1,414 (were 1,113): transform-origin 21 of 23 (were 3), context 15 of 18 (3), paint-order 12 of 14 (6), image 39 of 56 (25; most of the others are files it names, which are not read), linearGradient 50 of 51 (42), stop 32 of 32 (29), style 21 of 21 (18), use 42 of 42 (39), svg 39 of 48 (34). The 399 SVG files of icons and diagrams the renderer is checked on draw the same pixels as before. The command-line program grows by 0.75MB (16.9 to 17.7MB), mostly the tables of the encodings.
- SVG: `mix-blend-mode` (its sixteen modes and plus-lighter, by the W3C's Compositing and Blending) and `isolation`, read from CSS only as Chrome reads them; feBlend takes the modes of CSS too; a clip path's own `clip-path` and those of its children (each child clipped by its own), a clip path that refers to itself (the second time, no clip, as Chrome), `clip-rule` inherited where the clip path is; the root `svg` element's `clip-path`, `mask` and `opacity`. On the test suite of resvg (rendered at the size of its references), its clipPath tests pass 51 of 56 (were 36; the five others are text in fonts it ships), mix-blend-mode 21 of 21 (5), isolation 2 of 2 (1), feBlend 11 of 11 (9).
- Colour emoji, in `Text()`, watermarks and SVG: glyphs made of layers (the COLR table, version 0, with its CPAL palette: Segoe UI Emoji on Windows) are drawn in their colours, a layer of the text's colour in the text's colour, and the pictures of bitmap colour fonts (CBDT: Noto Color Emoji, on Linux; sbix: Apple Color Emoji) scaled to the size of the text (as Pillow's Lanczos filter; in SVG, sampled as an image element is), the text's transparency over them all; an emoji is taken from the first font that has it in colour when it is shown as an emoji by default (Unicode's Emoji_Presentation) or asked to be by U+FE0F, and sequences of emoji (joined by U+200D, with a skin tone, a keycap, flags) are shaped into the one glyph the font has for them. Noto Color Emoji and Apple Color Emoji, where they are installed, are among the fallback fonts on Linux and macOS. Next to Chrome, an SVG line of text with ❤️ ✨ 😀 👍🏽 👨‍👩‍👧 ☕ in Segoe UI Emoji inks the same span, and two lines in Noto Color Emoji (one turned by `rotate`, one half transparent) the same spans; in `Text()`, Noto Color Emoji within two pixels.
- Text shaped by the tables of its font, in `Text()`, watermarks and SVG, by HarfBuzz's shaper as ported to Go (`github.com/go-text/typesetting`): the scripts of India (Devanagari, Bengali, Tamil, ...), Thai, Lao, Tibetan, Myanmar, Khmer and the other scripts whose letters join, reorder or stack, Arabic and Persian with all the ligatures and marks of their fonts, and marks set on letters in any script, are drawn as the font has them; Latin, Chinese, Japanese, Korean and the like are laid out letter by letter as before, and their output does not change, unless their font makes ligatures or contextual forms of Latin text by default (as Calibri, Cambria, Consolas, Corbel, Candara and Constantia do; Arial, Segoe UI and Times New Roman do not): its text is then shaped too, as Chrome shapes it (a line of Calibri with its ffi and fi inks the same span as Chrome's; it was three pixels wider). Next to Chrome with the same fonts, the ink of lines of Hindi, Bengali, Tamil, Thai, Arabic and Persian lies within one pixel of Chrome's across, in `Text()` and in SVG. - Text of right-to-left scripts, in `Text()`, watermarks and SVG: the letters of Arabic and Persian joined as their neighbours have them (by the font's tables, as in the entry above, or where they cannot be read by Unicode's presentation forms, lam and alef as their ligature; a zero width non-joiner parting them and a joiner joining), and each line put in the order it is seen by the whole of the Unicode Bidirectional Algorithm (explicit embeddings, overrides and isolates, paired brackets, mirrored glyphs; a port of the reference implementation of Unicode as golang.org/x/text holds it, which gives the order of all 770,241 cases of Unicode 17's BidiTest.txt and all 91,707 of its BidiCharacterTest.txt), the levels resolved over the whole paragraph before it is broken into lines, a paragraph running the way its first strong character outside isolates does (in SVG, as the `direction` property says, which also turns `text-anchor`). - Characters drawn as nothing (joiners, marks of direction, embeddings and isolates, variation selectors as after an emoji, tags, the zero width space, the soft hyphen) take no room and draw no box in `Text()`, watermarks and SVG, as HarfBuzz and Chrome have them; they drew a glyph of the font or its missing box, and a variation selector took the room of a space. A line of `Text()` with a width may break at a zero width space, and at a soft hyphen, which then shows a hyphen.
- `Pages(images)` (`goimgtools pages image... out.pdf`): a PDF document of several pages, one image each, in order, each read through its own recipe while its page is written, so memory holds one page at a time whatever their number; read as an image, it is its first page. The operations and settings of a page (Resize, Density, ToProfile, ...) go on its image, on the document they are an error; WithContext on the document or on a page stops it. The command takes a last argument ending in .pdf, so that an image is not overwritten when the name of the PDF is forgotten.
- `ToProfile(icc)` (`-profile p3|adobe-rgb|file.icc`), as sharp's `withIccProfile`: the colours converted into an RGB profile of primaries and tone curves and the profile written with the output; from the input's own profile directly, so that colours sRGB cannot show are kept (a Display P3 red into Adobe RGB is LittleCMS's (239, 0, 0), not the (219, 0, 0) a way through sRGB clips it to); within one level of LittleCMS on 32³ colours; the result has 8 bits a channel, as with `ToSRGB`. `ProfileDisplayP3()` and `ProfileAdobeRGB()` give the profiles of those spaces. With tiles, or an animation `Animated` keeps, it is an error.
- ICNS, the icon file of macOS (`ICNS(ICNSOptions{Sizes})`, `.icns`, `-icns-sizes`): written as square PNG images of the standard sizes up to the image's, the image fitted in its proportions and centred on transparency (Pillow stretches it to every size, up to 1024), each type also standing for the Retina image of half its size, with a table of contents, as iconutil writes it; read is the largest image, a PNG one or one of the older run-length RGB images with its mask, as Pillow reads them, to the pixel.
- PDF output (`PDF(PDFOptions{Quality})`, `.pdf`, `-quality` for a JPEG page): a document of one page as large as the image at its resolution, the picture stored without loss (Flate, rows filtered as PNG filters them) or as a JPEG, its transparency as a soft mask, its colour profile kept (but for a profile of other channels than the picture, an RGB one on an image made grey); a page larger than Acrobat's 200 inches is made smaller, the picture keeping its pixels; written as the image is read (the soft mask waits in working storage). pdfium draws the pages as the images within one level.
- SVG input (`.svg`), drawn with this package's anti-aliased rasterizer a band of rows at a time, the memory it takes independent of the size drawn: shapes and paths (arcs, curves), transforms, nested viewports and viewBox, fills (non-zero, even-odd) and strokes (dashes; miter, round and bevel joins; butt, round and square caps), linear and radial gradients (focal point, spread, units, inheritance), opacity of shapes and groups, CSS (style attributes and style sheets: type, class, id, descendant and child selectors), use and symbol, switch (`requiredFeatures` and `requiredExtensions` taken as unsupported, as librsvg takes them, so that the labels of draw.io fall back to their text), clip paths (of shapes and of text), luminance and alpha masks (`mask-type`), patterns, markers, `paint-order`, `vector-effect: non-scaling-stroke`, text and tspan in the fonts installed (bold, italic, anchors, kerning, fallback for characters a font lacks; the font properties of any ancestor; letter-spacing and word-spacing, underline, overline and line-through, dominant-baseline and alignment-baseline from the font's own metrics as Chrome places them, baseline-shift, the rotation of each character, textLength by spacing or by stretching, and textPath, the glyphs along a path turned with it (librsvg draws none of the last four nor word-spacing)) and embedded pictures (data URIs; files are not read; one drawn at less than half its size, or smaller at all when the whole would not fit in half the memory left, decoded at the size it is drawn, by the average of the pixels each covers, as cairo shrinks it: a 24-megapixel photograph drawn at 600×400 in 0.25s, where librsvg refuses a data URI of more than 10MB). Shapes a use draws again elsewhere share their outlines: a matplotlib scatter plot of 20,000 markers takes 5MB of them and 0.6s. A resize the chain starts with draws the vector at its size, as sharp at any scale. Next to librsvg (sharp 0.35.5) the pictures differ only along edges (mean difference 0.04 to 0.27 levels on eight test documents, blurs 1.0). Filters: every primitive of the specification (Gaussian blur, offset, flood, composite, merge, colour matrix, blend, drop shadow, component transfer, turbulence (its reference noise, stitched tiles too), morphology, convolution, displacement, tile, image (a picture or an element), diffuse and specular lighting with distant, point and spot lights) and their subregions, in linear light as the specification says; next to librsvg within a level or two (its lighting colour is not taken into linear light, which the specification and Chrome do). The primitives are worked a band of rows at a time, each with the rows above and below its primitives reach, and the pictures of floats a primitive still to come does not read are given back as it goes: only the element's picture and the result are held whole, as bytes, so a 1400×1000 bevel or a 700×700 noise texture is worked at full resolution within the default 64MB (the same pixels as with unlimited memory, peak 60MB), where before it was worked at a lower resolution; that is left for regions even a band of a few rows of which would not fit in half the memory left (a filter that puts the element itself on top, a drop shadow, then draws it after, sharp, in linear light when it is painted in opaque colours). CSS custom properties (`--name` in any case, on `:root` too, `var(--name, fallback)`, as VS Code's icons use them), the `font` shorthand, font sizes in percentages of the parent's and CSS's keywords (`small` to `xx-large`, `larger`, `smaller`), the generic families as browsers choose them (Times New Roman, Arial, Consolas, ...), families of other systems as browsers put them (Helvetica as Arial, Courier as Consolas) and, after the families named, the default serif, as CSS says; white space is collapsed before the positions of x, y, dx, dy and rotate go to the characters, and a line of mixed scripts keeps the baseline and underline of its first font. Superscripts and subscripts are shifted by half the height of their font and overlines stand on the ascent, as Chrome draws them. Next to Chrome, the ink of 62 of 65 documents of text features lies within a pixel of Chrome's (the others: the default font of Chrome on a Chinese system, and its rounding of a font one unit high); a test compares a document of them with Chrome's picture. Documents that ask for too much (uses that multiply) stop at the memory limit.
- `ParseColor`, and so the command line and `Do`, take every CSS colour: the 148 names, `#rgba`, `rgb()`, `rgba()`, `hsl()`, `hsla()`.
- TGA, Truevision's Targa (`TGA()`, `.tga`), the format of game textures: colour-mapped, 15 to 32-bit colour and grey images read, stored or run-length compressed (a run may go on into the next row), from any corner, the alpha of TGA 2.0's extension area (none, or premultiplied) heeded; 16-bit pixels take their attribute bit as opacity only when the header gives one bit to it, set for opaque (Pillow takes a set bit for transparent, whatever the header), and their 5-bit channels widened as Pillow widens them (v × 255 / 31). Written run-length compressed from the top row, as Pillow writes with compression `"tga_rle"` and orientation 1, to the byte.
- QOI, the "Quite OK Image" format (`QOI()`, `.qoi`): lossless RGB or RGBA, read and written as a stream, the reference encoder's file and Pillow's to the byte (but that the colour space is marked sRGB, which Pillow does when told).
- Netpbm (`PNM()`, `.ppm`, `.pgm`, `.pbm`, `.pnm`): PBM, PGM and PPM in text or binary, PAM (with transparency) and PFM read, values of any maximum scaled as Pillow scales them (to 16 bits above 255, where Pillow keeps 8 for colour), a binary value above the maximum taken as the maximum and a text one refused, as by Pillow; PGM for a grey image and PPM for any other written as Pillow writes them, to the byte, with 16 bits when the image has them; transparency is laid over black, as for JPEG.
- Reading Photoshop files, PSD and PSB (`.psd`, `.psb`): the composite image Photoshop saves with the layers (the layers themselves are not read), in every colour mode (bitmap, grey, indexed, RGB, CMYK through its profile as other CMYK files, multichannel and duotone as grey, Lab as sRGB) at 1, 8, 16 and 32 bits (32-bit documents are linear light, put on the sRGB curve), stored or PackBits compressed, with its colour profile, EXIF, XMP and resolution, a band of rows at a time from the file. Its transparency is found as psd-tools finds it (a fourth channel that is a saved selection is not taken for it) and the colours are freed of the white Photoshop lays them on. Pillow reads 8-bit PSD only, takes any fourth channel of an RGB file for alpha and leaves the white in, and misreads the PackBits composite of files with extra channels (CMYK with spot colours); on the 82 files of psd-tools' tests it reads, the pixels are Pillow's wherever those differences do not apply. Spot colour channels are not laid on the composite.
- PNG level 10 (`PNGOptions{CompressionLevel: 10}`, `-png-level 10`), smaller than level 9 and much slower: each row's filter is chosen by compressing the rows with each of the five (libdeflate's level 10, with the 32KB before as the dictionary; the five share the work of entering it, and are compressed on the threads free), the three versions of every 256KB of rows (the rows' own filters, Brute's, none) are compressed too, to keep the smallest, and the data is compressed with a Go port of Zopfli (`internal/zopfli`, byte for byte Zopfli's output with its settings; here 60 runs of its optimal parse, and for an image of less than 1MB of data as many as take the time of 1MB, up to 200 (the 5 of the 17 under 1MB 0.013% smaller, one 0.07%), with what ECT adds to it as options: runs priced by the code lengths after them, no limit on the blocks, the codes and header of each block chosen among more, short matches written as literals where that is smaller; 0.026% smaller; and, not in ECT, `FineSplit`: the data split further where its symbols' statistics change, before the parse (on the greedy parse Zopfli splits by, so that each part is parsed with its own statistics) and after it, the points found in one pass by the entropy of the symbols on either side and an estimate of the headers, where Zopfli's search, trying 9 points a round, misses a short stretch such as an icon's transparent rows, and rows alternating between two kinds every few kilobytes; 0.14% smaller, a photograph 0.46%) in its master blocks of 1MB, each on a thread of its own, the parts Zopfli splits a block into on the threads free, all joined bit by bit into the stream Zopfli writes for the whole; a block that level 9's compressor makes smaller is written as that; a palette is written in the order its colours first appear, as ECT writes it, without the colours no pixel uses (the pixels are the same). On 17 screenshots, photographs and game artwork: 0.39% smaller than level 9 and 0.23% smaller than ECT 0.9.5 `-9` (smaller on 15 of them), in 438s with 8 threads (level 9 31.5s, ECT 606s on 6 cores). Each thread compressing takes about 50MB: with the default memory limit it compresses on one thread. The port writes Zopfli's bytes in a third of Zopfli's time (1MB of a photograph, 60 runs, one core: 10.5s against 29s): it keeps all of each position's matches from the first run of the optimal parse, where Zopfli keeps 8 and searches again for the rest in every run, and looks up the costs of lengths and distances once a run. - `pipeline.Env.TryGo`: `Go` that never runs the work on the calling goroutine, for work a stage can put off until a helper is free while it goes on with its own.
- Near-lossless WebP (`WebPOptions{NearLossless: true}`, `-webp-near-lossless`), as libwebp's (sharp's `nearLossless`, `cwebp -near_lossless`), ported from its predictor (`predictor_enc.c`): where a pixel differs from one of its four neighbours by more than a little, the residual of each channel is rounded to a multiple of a power of two below that difference, and the pixel becomes what the decoder will make of it, so the errors do not add up; smooth areas stay exact. `Quality` says how much: 100 nothing (the lossless file), 80 to 99 at most 1 level a channel, 60 to 79 2, 40 to 59 4, 20 to 39 8, below 16; the default 75. Each block's predictor is chosen by the cost of its rounded residuals, as libwebp chooses it, and a band of rows waits for the row below its last, so every pixel is judged by all four neighbours. A variant that stays lossless runs beside the others and wins where a pattern repeats exactly (one of 86 bytes lossless, 1176 rounded). Rounded by 4 and more, the predictor's blocks are 8×8 pixels rather than 4×4 (its modes weigh more against rounded residuals: 0.24% smaller). At 60: 6 Kodak photographs 37.0% smaller than lossless and 2.9% smaller than `cwebp -near_lossless 60` (libwebp 1.6.0), 17 screenshots, photographs and game artwork with them 7.3% smaller than cwebp (6.6% at 80; per image from 6.6% larger, a 5KB icon, to 13.4% smaller), in 25% more time than lossless; a 12-megapixel photograph in 8.6s against 3.7s on one core (cwebp takes 2.6 times its lossless time), at 22MB (lossless 23 to 25MB). - `Blank(width, height, colour)`: an image of one colour, as sharp's `create` and Pillow's `Image.new` make one, a canvas for `Composite`; its rows are made as they are read (a 6000×4000 canvas written as JPEG within a 4MB memory limit). `Info.Format` is `"blank"` for it.
- `JPEGOptions.OvershootDeringing` (`-jpeg-overshoot-deringing`), as mozjpeg's overshoot deringing and sharp's `overshootDeringing`, byte for byte sharp's (32 files of screenshots and photographs at qualities 30 to 90, 18 of synthetic text, saturated colours and noise in 4:2:0 and 4:4:4): in a block with pixels at 255 beside others, those are raised above 255 along a Catmull-Rom curve over each run of them in zig-zag order (capped by the DC quantizer and so that the block's mean stays at most 255) before the transform, so that a hard edge against white rings less; a block it changes goes through the integer transform, the curve in float32 with no fused operations, the same on every processor. - `WebPOptions.AlphaQuality` (`-webp-alpha-quality`), as libwebp's `alpha_q` and sharp's `alphaQuality`: the transparency of lossy WebP reduced to fewer levels before it is coded (2 to 16 up to quality 70, up to 256 at 99), chosen for the image by k-means over its values as libwebp's `QuantizeLevels` chooses them (ported in float64 with no fused operations, the same on every processor), 0 and 255 kept; the ALPH chunk says so. The alpha chunks of 6 images with transparency: 35.1KB at 50 and 24.2KB at 20 against 71.1KB exact (libwebp 1.6: 36.5KB, 23.9KB and 112.5KB). 0 and 100 keep it exactly, the default.

### Changed

- Rotation by a quarter turn (`Rotate(90)`, `Rotate(270)`, and the EXIF orientations that exchange width and height): each round of rows goes to the store as one block, the strips of the result put together from the blocks, instead of a write for every row of the result each round (140,000 writes for 24 megapixels): a 6000×4000 JPEG turned and written again takes 0.31s instead of 0.68s on one thread, in the same 14MB, the same bytes out.
- Blur and the unsharp mask (`Blur`, `Sharpen` with a sigma) on x86-64: the box passes along the rows take the sum of each window from running sums of the row (AVX2, two pixels at a time), where they slid the window a pixel at a time. A 6000×4000 JPEG is blurred with sigma 2 in 0.27s instead of 0.31s on one thread and in 0.12s instead of 0.14s on four, and sharpened with sigma 2 in 0.35s instead of 0.38s; every byte and the peak memory are the same.
- Animated GIF output allocates less: the frames are stored once while the palette is chosen (they were stored a second time for the case that each frame gets a palette of its own, which is now written from the same rows), so a small animation no longer needs a temporary file for the second copy; and the buffers that were made anew for every frame, or for every trial of how a frame codes smallest, are used again (the strip a GIF frame is decoded into and its LZW reader, the strip the picture so far is handed on in, the tables of the trial encoder: 64KB some fifteen times a frame). An animation of 18 frames of 768×512 resized to 384 wide allocates 18MB in all instead of 48MB. The file is the same to the byte; time and peak memory of the whole program are as they were (0.71s, 23MB: see the comparison for why).
- GIF decoding is about 2.2 times as fast: the LZW data of a frame is decoded by a reader of this package's own instead of `compress/lzw`'s, which takes the bits from the buffered block eight bytes at a time and writes what each code stands for straight into the rows, eight bytes at a time (each code keeps the last bytes of its string as one word, as Wuffs' decoder does), where the standard reader writes it backwards into a buffer of its own and copies it twice. The pixels, the errors and what is read of the file are the same for any input (checked against the standard reader on 6,000 streams: whole, cut short, with bits changed, with data after their end, written with and without clearing a full table). A 6000×4000 GIF to a JPEG 1536 wide takes 0.17s instead of 0.24s on one thread (libvips 0.19s) and 0.12s instead of 0.13s on four.
- SVG: an element's properties are looked up in its `style` attribute without parsing the attribute again for each one: an SVG of 30,000 paths styled as Inkscape writes them draws in 0.70s instead of 1.34s, at a peak of 113MB instead of 150MB (with `-mem 512`), to the same pixels.
- Lossless WebP of at most 16 colours: the pixels unpacked, without the predictor, race against the colour table's packed ones, and are written when 5% smaller before the split into Huffman groups: packing makes a repeat start and end on a whole symbol, which smooth pictures of a few levels lose by (alpha planes reduced to 2 to 12 levels 5.6% smaller, one 9%; with `AlphaQuality` 50 and 20 the alpha chunks 2.7% smaller). Such images take up to about 5 times as long when the unpacked pixels win.
- WebP no longer keeps the colour of fully transparent pixels by default, as libwebp, sharp and Pillow do not (`WebPOptions.Exact` keeps it): lossless output stores them as transparent black and, where the predictor follows, as the colour their neighbours predict (libwebp's `WebPReplaceTransparentPixels` and the cleaning in its predictor), lossy output smooths their samples out, as before (with `Exact` it no longer does). What can be seen is the same; a cut-out photograph with noise under its transparency is 27% smaller lossless, icons and drawings change by about 1%, either way. Opaque images give the same files.
- PNG levels 8 and 9 split each block of deflate further where the statistics of its symbols change, as level 10 does (`FineSplit` of the deflate port, in integer arithmetic, so that the file stays the same on every processor), with no limit of 15 parts (0.018% (8) and 0.014% (9) smaller, a photograph whose rows alternate between two kinds every few kilobytes 0.044% (9)), and write each block with the smallest of more codes and ways of writing its header, as ECT does (`MoreCodes`: the counts as they are, evened out as zopfli and as Brotli even them, limited to 14 bits down to 9; the run codes 16, 17 and 18 used or not; 0.019% (8) and 0.015% (9) smaller): the 17 screenshots, photographs and game artwork 0.037% (8) and 0.029% (9) smaller in all, in the same time; level 9 is 0.16% larger than ECT `-9` instead of 0.19%.
- PNG levels 8 to 10 write the image data in IDAT chunks of 1MB (a stream of up to that in one chunk, as ECT and oxipng write it) instead of chunks of 32KB or of the size of the compressor's larger writes. Each chunk takes 12 bytes: the 17 screenshots, photographs and game artwork are 0.023% smaller at level 9 (0.19% larger than ECT `-9` instead of 0.21%) and 0.007% at level 10; the compressed data is the same. Up to 1MB more memory at these levels, less for smaller images.
- `pipeline.Env.Parallel` also gives calls to helpers that become free while calls are left (the calling goroutine looks for them between its calls), rather than only to those free when it starts.
- No dependency on `github.com/klauspost/compress`: the dependencies are `golang.org/x/image`, `x/text` and `x/sys` only. With Go 1.27 or later, PNG data of more than one block, at levels 1 to 6, is compressed by Go 1.27's `compress/flate`, copied into the package (`internal/gflate`, with a method to reset it with a dictionary and, on little-endian processors, word loads as `klauspost/compress` makes them): the same bytes as 0.2.0 (with Go 1.26 too, which compresses every block with its own `compress/flate` as before), at about the same speed (a picture of 6000×4000 at level 6, 720ms against 707ms on one core, 276ms against 268ms on four). Compressed text and ICC profiles in PNG files are read by `compress/zlib`.
- Lossy WebP decoding shares its work out with `Threads(4)` and above, as libwebp's threaded decoder: the next band of macroblock rows is decoded while the last one is used, and each row is reconstructed and filtered while the next is parsed; the pixels are the same. A 6000×4000 WebP (quality 80) shrunk on load to fit 1536 with `MethodLibvips`: 0.21s on five threads against 0.27s, as fast as sharp (0.21s; its libwebp decodes on a second thread).
- Lossless WebP: Huffman groups whose codes cost more than they save are joined, as libwebp's greedy combining of histograms does, and every block then chooses again among the groups left (images of up to 4 megapixels). The 24 Kodak photographs 0.4% smaller at the default effort and 0.8% at effort 9, 17 screenshots, photographs and game artwork 0.1%, 30 Windows icons 0.7%; a higher effort no longer gives larger files of photographs (effort 7 had been 0.5% larger than the default, effort 4 than effort 3). The repeated passes of the grouping go through each block's symbols, kept by the first, when they fit below the peak of the memory budget and in a sixteenth of it: the Kodak photographs in 11.2s instead of 13.4s at the default effort on one core, 18.6s instead of 24.0s at effort 9. Images larger than 4 megapixels take the same time and memory as before.
- Lossless WebP at effort 4 has the optimal parse, as from effort 5: screenshots, drawings and game artwork up to 12% smaller (17 such images and photographs 5.2% smaller in all, in 25s instead of 9.5s on one core, against 42s at the default effort), 30 Windows icons 2.6%, photographs 0.2% (the 24 Kodak photographs in 5.2s instead of 3.4s).
- Lossless WebP shares its work out with `Threads(4)` and above beyond the predictor search: the variants raced against each other take each row at the same time, and the optimal parse searches a stretch of 8K pixels on several cores (the whole stretch entered into the hash chains first, each search starting from its own pixel's link, the links the stretch overwrites read from a copy, so the matches are those of searching one pixel after the other), and the replays of the Huffman grouping choose each block's group on several cores. The same bytes. At the default effort on eight threads: 17 screenshots, photographs and game artwork in 21s instead of 41–43s, a palette screenshot of 3426×1912 in 5.6s instead of 14s, the 24 Kodak photographs in 7.1s instead of 11.1–11.6s; 2 to 7MB more at the peak.

- Colour reduction (GIF, PNG with a palette) shares its k-means passes out with `Threads(4)` and above; the result is the same. The 24 Kodak photographs to GIF: 2.0s on six threads against 3.0s; a 6000×4000 JPEG shrunk to 1536 and written as GIF, 0.38s against 0.56s.

### Removed

- HEIC, AVIF and JPEG XL are not supported. ## 0.2.0 (2026-11-20)

### Upgrading from 0.1.0

This release settles the API: from it on, exported names are only added to, never removed or changed (`apidiff` checks every push against it). To get there, a few names of 0.1.0 changed:

| 0.1.0 | Now |
|---|---|
| `JPEG(85)` | `JPEG(JPEGOptions{Quality: 85})`, as `PNG`, `WebP`, `GIF` and `TIFF` take their settings (`JPEG()` is quality 80) |
| `JPEG(85, JPEGOptions{Progressive: true})` | `JPEG(JPEGOptions{Quality: 85, Progressive: true})` |
| `Rotate()` | `AutoOrient()` |
| `Rotate(90)` | `Rotate(90)`, which now takes any angle in degrees (a `float64`) and `RotateOptions` |
| `Extract(left, top, w, h)` | `Crop(left, top, w, h)` |
| `ResizeOptions{Filter: &Filter{...}}` | `ResizeOptions{CustomKernel: &CustomKernel{...}}` |
| `Info.HasProfile` | `Info.HasICC` |

The operations that can follow libvips (sharp) or Pillow share one type for it, `Method` (`MethodDefault`, `MethodLibvips`, `MethodPillow`), in `ResizeOptions`, `BlurOptions`, `SharpenOptions`, `RotateOptions` and `Convolution`.

What can still surprise code written for 0.1.0:

- `Animated()` returns an error wrapping `errors.ErrUnsupported` for an animation it cannot keep (an animated GIF, WebP or PNG to JPEG, BMP or TIFF), where 0.1.0 silently wrote the first frame; to PNG it writes an APNG. Leave out `Animated()` to get the first frame.
- A lossy WebP is decoded to `*image.NRGBA` strips, no longer to `*image.YCbCr`, so that its colours are libwebp's; `Metadata()` still reports `ycbcr`. Code that type-asserts the decoded image should accept any `image.Image`.
- `Frames().LoopCount` of a GIF is the number of times it plays, as documented, one more than 0.1.0 returned.
- The default PNG level is 7 (6 before), compressed as libdeflate compresses at its level 7: smaller files, written more slowly, with about 2MB more working memory (less for small images); under a smaller `MemoryLimit` the run fails with `ErrMemoryLimit`. `PNGOptions{CompressionLevel: 6}` keeps the old files and needs less.
- `Threads(n)` is no longer capped at 3: a larger n uses that many cores.
- JPEG files are written as libjpeg-turbo writes them (Pillow's and sharp's bytes), no longer as `image/jpeg` does: at the same quality, pixels a level or so apart, files of about the same size.
- `Flatten` of an image whose colours are not multiplied by alpha (a PNG, a WebP; most images), and JPEG output of an image with transparency (flattened over black), take the colours as libvips does, `(colour×alpha + background×(255−alpha)) / 255` with the fraction dropped, so that they are sharp's to the byte; they were a level apart on some pixels.
- A CMYK file (JPEG, TIFF) without a colour profile is converted to sRGB through libvips's CMYK profile, as libvips and sharp convert it, no longer with `color.CMYK`'s plain formula: printing inks come out as printed (full cyan is RGB 0,153,228, no longer 0,255,255). A `*image.CMYK` given to `FromImage` keeps `color.CMYK`'s values.

### Changed

- PNG at the default level, 7, is compressed with a port of libdeflate's lazy parsing (its level 7, to the byte), the same with every Go version, instead of `compress/flate`. Against Go 1.27's level 7, on 126 images: 1.8% smaller (geometric mean; from 28% smaller to 2.5% larger), in 19% more time end to end on one core. Paletted images (after colour reduction, or from a paletted source) gain most, as compress/flate's level 7 did poorly on their rows: 256-colour PNGs of photographs 2.4–3% smaller, of screenshots 4.6–8%, of smooth gradients 3–25% (one without dithering 2.3 times smaller). The same file whatever the number of threads and the memory limit. With several threads and a tight memory limit, PNG compression at levels 7 to 9 now waits for a block to be done where it failed for want of room for another. The memory the compressor takes is counted in the limit, and is sized for the image: a 150×103 image needs 0.8MB, a large one 2.3MB.
- `FastShrinkOnLoad` on a JPEG (and on a lossy WebP): the rest of the resize, which scales the Y, Cb and Cr planes, runs with AVX2 (NEON on arm64): the products of the 14-bit weights and the samples in pairs of 16-bit multiplications, eight destination samples at a time across and sixteen down, the same whole numbers, so the same bytes. A 6000×4000 JPEG fitted into 1536 is decoded and resized in 70ms instead of 106ms on one core (sharp: 77ms), and written as a WebP thumbnail in 140ms instead of 179ms (sharp, at its default effort, which is 4 to this module's 5: 149ms); a 4:4:4 one, to a JPEG, in 160ms instead of 212ms.
- GIF output and PNG colour reduction compare colours as libimagequant (pngquant's library, which sharp uses) does: in its gamma (each channel to the power 1.21) and with green weighted above red and blue (1, 0.5, 0.45), where all three counted alike; and the dithering passes on an error larger than 10 levels at three quarters, as libimagequant damps its large errors, so that they no longer add up over the pixels after them into specks of the wrong colour. On 12 photographs reduced to 256 colours as GIF, SSIMULACRA2 75.6 → 81.3 and butteraugli 1.66 → 1.07, files 2% smaller (sharp: 76.9 and 1.64); the 8-megapixel photograph as PNG of 256 colours 70.9 → 78.5, 4% smaller, and at a quarter of its size 82.3 against pngquant's 79.5 in an 11% smaller file; an animated GIF reduced to 128 colours 76.8 → 82.8. PSNR, which counts the channels alike, can fall by up to 1.5dB. 1–2% more time.
- JPEG files are written as libjpeg-turbo writes them, to the byte (sharp's files as they are, Pillow's without their JFIF segment): libjpeg's colour conversion, downsampling, accurate integer forward DCT, dummy blocks past the edges, optimized Huffman tables and markers, at the same speed. A resolution is written as JFIF 1.01, as libjpeg writes it.
- `FastShrinkOnLoad` applies to a lossy WebP without alpha too: reduced to a third or less, it is decoded at twice the size of the result, as libwebp scales while decoding, and the kernel does the rest (a 6000×4000 one to fit 400×400 in 0.39s instead of 0.74s, 48–51dB from the result without the option).
- `FastShrinkOnLoad` reduces the blocks with libjpeg's reduced inverse DCTs (`jidctred.c`) instead of averages worked out afresh, at the same speed: the planes of a 4:4:4 or grey JPEG are those of libjpeg's scaled decoding (Pillow's `draft`), and the result as close to a resize of the full image (46.7–50.5dB).
- JPEG files are decoded as libjpeg-turbo decodes them, and so Pillow and sharp, to the byte (its accurate integer inverse DCT, its fancy upsampling of subsampled chroma, its YCbCr to RGB tables), instead of as `image/jpeg` does: subsampled colour edges are smoother (a 4:2:2 photograph 0.36 dB closer to the original), and every operation that gives Pillow's or sharp's pixels does so on JPEG input too. Colour JPEG strips are RGBA rather than YCbCr, but where a JPEG goes straight into a JPEG or lossy WebP file.
- TIFF decoding: LZW 1.5 times faster (a 6000×4000 RGBA image in 0.45s instead of 0.68s; libtiff 0.31s), with the same bytes and errors as `x/image` on any input (fuzzed), and Deflate blocks read 64KB at a time (0.26s instead of 0.30s; libtiff 0.27s).
- `FitCover` puts a centred crop where sharp does: when the image is an odd number of pixels larger than the box, the odd pixel is cut on the left (top) rather than the right (bottom).
- JPEG encoding: the SIMD kernels write the quantized coefficients in zig-zag order themselves, and the mask of the nonzero ones is taken with AVX2: a 6000×4000 photograph at quality 85 in 87ms instead of 100ms on one core, with optimized tables 151ms instead of 168ms.
- JPEG encoding with AVX2: the forward DCT works in 16-bit lanes, two rows of samples to a register, as libjpeg-turbo's jfdctint-avx2 does (pairs of samples multiplied and added in one instruction), where it worked in 32-bit lanes with a slower multiply; the quantization divides as libjpeg-turbo's jquanti-avx2 divides, with two 16-bit high multiplies, which a test checks give the rounded quotient for every coefficient up to 32767 and every quantization value; the zig-zag order is made with byte shuffles of the rows in registers, where 64 coefficients were moved one at a time. The same bytes. A 6000×4000 RGB image in memory, quality 85: 70ms instead of 74ms on one core, 107ms instead of 112ms with optimized tables.
- Lossless WebP decoding: each pixel goes into the colour cache as it is decoded (a literal at once, a copy once copied), where the pixels since the last cache lookup were entered before each one, and a pixel taken from the cache is not entered again (it is already where it would go); a code of a single symbol (the alpha of an opaque image) is taken without a lookup, so that the next code does not wait for one; the inverse predictors 0 to 4 and 8 to 12 run four pixels at a time with AVX on amd64, after libwebp's SSE2 code (Select with a sum of absolute differences, the clamped one in 16-bit lanes), and the green is added back two pixels at a time. The same pixels. A 6000×4000 photograph: 0.31s instead of 0.42s on one core, where libwebp 1.6's `dwebp` with its SIMD takes 0.32s; a 2.5-megapixel one 24ms instead of 32ms (25ms).
- Progressive JPEG: the scan search codes every candidate scan of every block going from one coefficient that is not zero at the scan's bit plane to the next, as libjpeg-turbo's encoder does, instead of looking at each coefficient in turn; which ones those are is worked out without a branch (with AVX2 on amd64, 64 coefficients at once), as is the last nonzero coefficient of each block. The same bytes. A 6000×4000 RGB image in memory, quality 85: 0.75s instead of 1.85s on one core (mozjpeg's search through sharp: 1.17s).
- An image in memory (`FromImage`) is encoded without copying its rows: it is handed on in strips of 16 rows (where 16 rows take no more than 1MB), so that the bands of the JPEG and WebP encoders lie inside one strip and are passed on as views of it, where they were copied out of strips of 5 rows; whether an `*image.RGBA` or `*image.NRGBA` has transparency is checked eight pixels at a time, and the JPEG encoder asks only of NRGBA images, for which it matters. A 6000×4000 `*image.RGBA` written as JPEG (quality 85) in 74ms instead of 87ms on one core. The bytes are the same.
- Lossy WebP decoding: the coefficients are read by assembly on amd64 and arm64 (plain integer instructions, so also with Go 1.26 on arm64; the purego tag keeps the Go code), the range renormalized from tables after every bit instead of only when it falls below 127, which took an unpredictable branch, and the bits that end a block or skip a zero going straight to their code; near the end of the read window, where a block could need bytes not yet read, the Go code reads it as before, so errors come at the same rows. A block whose only coefficient is the DC (about half of them in a photograph) is transformed by adding the DC, with AVX2 or NEON, as libwebp does, where every block with a coefficient went through the whole inverse transform; the DC, vertical and horizontal predictions write whole rows at once. On amd64 the loop filter works as libwebp's SSE2 code does, in 8-bit lanes with saturating arithmetic, the three inner edges of a macroblock's luma in one call that keeps the columns (or rows) it has loaded for the next edge, where each edge was loaded, widened to 16 bits and transposed on its own: a third of the time. Every pixel is the same. A 6000×4000 photograph (quality 80) decodes on one core in 0.25s instead of 0.32s (`dwebp`, libwebp 1.6 with its SIMD: 0.256s), with the purego tag in 0.51s instead of 0.53s; shrunk on load to fit 400×400, 0.27s instead of 0.39s.
- `Modulate` keeps what it has turned each colour into, in a direct-mapped cache of 32K colours per thread (256KB, within the memory limit): a photograph has a few hundred thousand colours among its millions of pixels, and nine in ten are looked up rather than worked out again, the same pixels. The JPEG encoder takes opaque NRGBA strips (from `Modulate`, sharpening, CLAHE and other operations) as RGBA again, the same bytes, as re-cutting them into bands had lost that they are opaque. A 6000×4000 photograph with `Modulate(1.1, 1.3, 20)`, single-threaded: 0.41s instead of 0.71s.
- JPEG encoding: the Huffman codes and value bits of a block go into a 64-bit buffer that goes out eight bytes at a time (as libjpeg-turbo's put_buffer), for the standard and for optimized tables, the same bytes: a 6000×4000 photograph at quality 85 in 78ms instead of 85ms on one core, with optimized tables 118ms instead of 151ms (libjpeg-turbo through Pillow: 66ms and 167ms). Lossy WebP decoding reads its bits as libwebp's VP8GetBitAlt does (no renormalizing while the range stays large), 1% faster.
- Lossy WebP at effort 1 is no longer the same as effort 2: it picks the 16x16 and chroma predictors by the error of their prediction (and the cost of the mode) and codes only those, as libwebp's methods 0 to 2 pick them, and codes two rather than four 4x4 modes of each block. A 6000×4000 photograph in 1.52s instead of 2.12s on one core; on 14 images, files 2.5% larger at the same SSIMULACRA2 and 4.0% at the same butteraugli than effort 2.
- Lossy WebP from colours (PNG, resized or flattened images; not a JPEG's Y'CbCr): sharp YUV shares the columns of each step among the helpers and runs its upsampling and corrections with AVX2 or NEON, every sample libwebp's as before: a 6000×4000 photograph flattened onto white, at quality 75, in 4.7s instead of 5.2s on one core, 1.2s instead of 1.8s with 8 threads.
- Lossy WebP decoding: the inverse transform with AVX2 or NEON (the kernel the encoder reconstructs with), every byte the same: a 6000×4000 photograph decodes in 0.33s instead of 0.36s on one core.
- `Blur`, the unsharp mask, `Median(3)` and `Trim` with AVX2 or NEON, every byte the same: the box passes of the blur (Pillow's integer arithmetic) across a row four channels at a time and down the rows eight columns at a time, the 3×3 median 32 bytes at a time. Rotation by any angle reads the pixels it interpolates straight from the tile they are in. A 6000×4000 photograph, single-threaded: `Blur(2)` adds 0.14s instead of 0.48s (Pillow 0.38s), `Sharpen(SharpenOptions{Sigma: 2})` 0.22s instead of 0.57s (0.48s), `Trim(10)` 0.09s instead of 0.91s, `RotateBy(30)` 0.51s instead of 1.13s (with the encoding of its larger output).
- The default resize (`x/image`'s arithmetic, in float64) with AVX2 or NEON: the sums down and across take the four channels of a pixel at once, each product rounded and then added (never fused) in the order of the Go code, and RGBA rows are turned into float64 the same way; every sum is the same to the bit, so the pixels are too. A 6000×4000 JPEG fitted into 1536×1536 and written as a JPEG in 0.17s instead of 0.25s on one core; 1080p enlarged to 4K in 0.09s instead of 0.12s.
- Lossy WebP encoding: the 4x4 forward transform (after libwebp's FTransform_SSE2), the inverse transform, the quantization and the squared error of the mode search, and the histogram of coefficients of the analysis, run with AVX2 or NEON; the trellis keeps the two nodes of each coefficient in registers with the path to each as a bit mask, and counts the bits of the block as it goes; the bits of a block are summed without branches. Every number is that of the Go code, so the files are the same: a 6000×4000 photograph at quality 80 in 4.4s instead of 6.5s on one core at the default effort, 2.6s instead of 4.0s at effort 1.
- `Resize` with the default method keeps 16-bit samples: a 16-bit PNG or TIFF is scaled to 16-bit strips (`*image.RGBA64`, `*image.Gray16`), the values `x/image`'s `Scale` gives such a destination, and written as 16 bits; it was cut to 8 bits.
- PNG encoding: the five filters of each row (and the sums by which libpng's heuristic chooses one) are worked out with AVX2 or NEON, the same row and choice as before: a 6000×4000 photograph to PNG (level 7) in 1.43s instead of 1.73s on one core.
- NEON on arm64 (with Go 1.27 or later; the `purego` build tag leaves it out): every AVX2 kernel (JPEG decoding and encoding, lossy WebP decoding, the search of loop filter levels, `MethodLibvips`' reduce) has a NEON version, every byte that of the Go code.
- Lossy WebP at effort 3 weighs the 4x4 modes without the trellis and keeps it for the mode chosen (libwebp's method 5): 15% faster than effort 4, which it was the same as, for files about 1% larger at the same SSIMULACRA2 and butteraugli (14 images).
- Lossy WebP encoding with AVX2 (amd64; the `purego` build tag leaves it out): the search of loop filter levels filters each macroblock with the decoder's AVX2 loop filter and takes the SSIM sums of its windows with AVX2, every number that of the Go code, so the files are the same: a 6000×4000 photograph at quality 80 in 6.5s instead of 8.3s on one core.
- Resizing with AVX2 (amd64; the `purego` build tag leaves it out): `MethodLibvips`'s reduce down and across, and libjpeg's 4×4 inverse DCT of a JPEG shrunk on load, run as assembly, every byte that of the Go code (and so sharp's); NRGBA strips of an image without transparency are encoded as JPEG through the same conversion as RGBA. A 6000×4000 JPEG fitted into 1536×1536 (Lanczos 3, `FastShrinkOnLoad`) and written as a JPEG: 109ms instead of 196ms on one core (sharp: 92ms).
- JPEG encoding with AVX2 (amd64; the `purego` build tag leaves it out): the conversion of RGB to YCbCr, the downsampling of the chroma, the forward DCT and the quantization run as assembly, every byte that of the Go code; and the first pass of optimized Huffman tables stages the symbols (a byte each) instead of a scan coded with the standard tables, which the second pass had to decode. A 6000×4000 photograph at quality 85: 100ms instead of 213ms on one core (libjpeg-turbo through Pillow: 66ms); with optimized tables 168ms instead of 388ms (167ms).
- JPEG decoding with AVX2 (amd64; the `purego` build tag leaves it out): the inverse DCT and the conversion of colour, with the upsampling of the chroma, run as assembly after libjpeg-turbo's SIMD code, every byte that of the Go code: a 6000×4000 photograph (quality 90) decodes on one core in 99ms (4:2:0), 109ms (4:2:2) and 126ms (4:4:4) instead of 208, 226 and 250ms, as fast as libjpeg-turbo with its SIMD (through Pillow: 101, 110 and 121ms).
- JPEG decoding is 45% faster, with the same pixels: the entropy-coded data is read 64 bits at a time and decoded through 11-bit tables that give most coefficients' values together with their codes, coefficients are dequantized as they are decoded, and the inverse DCT skips rows of zeros (a 6000×4000 baseline photograph in 98ms instead of 188ms on one core). With `FastShrinkOnLoad` the image is decoded straight to 1/2, 1/4 or 1/8 of its size from the coefficients, as libjpeg does, instead of being decoded in full and averaged, and the rest of the resize scales its Y, Cb and Cr planes as they are, in fixed point, instead of converting to RGBA: shrinking that photograph to fit 400×400 takes 82ms instead of 231ms (libvips' `vipsthumbnail`: 97ms), and to fit 1536×1536 157ms instead of 320ms (148ms; with 2 threads 108ms against its 172ms).
- A progressive JPEG read from a file or from memory is decoded with its scans in step, a row of blocks at a time, instead of storing the coefficients of the whole image (72MB at 6000×4000, which went to a temporary file under the default limit), and refinement scans visit only the coefficients that are not zero: a 6000×4000 photograph is decoded about three times as fast (0.24s instead of 0.70s on one core), with no temporary file and 0.5MB more memory. Shrunk by 8 with `FastShrinkOnLoad`, where each block becomes the mean of its pixels, only the scans of DC coefficients are decoded: that photograph shrunk to fit 400×400 in 50ms instead of 745ms (libvips' `vipsthumbnail`: 228ms). An end-of-band run that a corrupt scan leaves unfinished now ends with the scan, as in libjpeg; the standard library carries it into the next scan.
- JPEG encoding is more than twice as fast, writing the same bytes as before (those of the standard library's `jpeg.Encode`): the entropy-coded data is gathered 64 bits at a time and written 32 at a time, a block's bits kept in registers; only the coefficients that are not zero are visited, found four at a time; quantization has no branch; the colour conversion of RGBA and NRGBA pixels is done inline; and the chroma of a YCbCr image subsampled 4:2:0 (as decoded JPEGs mostly are) is taken as it is instead of being spread over every pixel and averaged back (4:4:4: averaged straight from its planes). A 6000×4000 JPEG re-encoded on one core: 0.23s instead of 0.54s (libvips: 0.12s; with 2 threads 0.13s against its 0.13s, with 4 threads 0.10s).
- PNG images and Deflate-compressed TIFF images are decompressed about twice as fast (`internal/inflate`, written with libdeflate's method: a 64-bit bit buffer refilled without a branch, tables that give a code's extra bits with it, the next code looked up before the refill, matches copied eight bytes at a time), accepting and rejecting the same data as before and reading as much of the file. A 6000×4000 RGB PNG is decoded in 0.25s instead of 0.37s on one core (libvips: 0.25s); converted to JPEG, 0.39s instead of 0.51s (0.25s).
- PNG rows filtered with Paeth are undone with the channels of a pixel together and without branches (the standard library undoes one channel along the row, then the next): a 6000×4000 RGBA photograph shrunk to fit 1536×1536 in 0.45s instead of 0.51s on one core, 0.26s instead of 0.32s with 2 threads (vipsthumbnail: 0.43s).
- Lossy WebP encoding at the default effort is 25% faster, writing the same files: the coded tokens kept between the two passes are read back as libwebp's bit reader reads (56 bits at a time), the SSIM sums by which the loop filter levels are chosen are taken over the fixed 16×16 and 8×8 blocks directly, and the trellis looks its cost tables up once per coefficient. A 6000×4000 photograph: 7.5s instead of 10.1s on one core, 1.55s instead of 1.79s with 8 threads.
- Lossy WebP decoding with AVX2 (amd64; the `purego` build tag leaves it out): the loop filter, and the upsampling and conversion of colour, run as assembly after libwebp's SSE2 code, every byte that of the Go code: a 6000×4000 photograph decodes on one core in 0.36s instead of 0.57s, faster than libwebp 1.6 with its SIMD (0.50s through Pillow).
- Lossy WebP decoding is 15–18% faster, with the same pixels: the bits are read as libwebp's VP8GetBit reads them, and a block's coefficients with the reader held in local variables (a 6000×4000 image at quality 95: 0.78s instead of 0.95s; dwebp 0.48s).
- Lossy WebP decoding shares its work when there are cores for it (`Threads` of 4 or more): the loop filter of each row of macroblocks runs while the next row is decoded, as libwebp's threaded decoder does, and the conversion to RGB is shared out; the pixels are the same. A 6000×4000 photograph shrunk to fit 1536×1536 in 0.49s instead of 0.57s with 4 threads.
- Lossless WebP decoding (and the compressed alpha of lossy WebP) takes 25–65% less time, with the same pixels and the same failures as `golang.org/x/image/webp`: the Huffman codes are decoded through tables, as libwebp decodes them (a code that is not complete keeps the tree), 64 bits of input at a time, and the predictor transform works on a run of pixels four channels at a time (a 1600×1097 photograph in 31ms instead of 42ms on one core, a 1001×687 one in 15ms instead of 22ms; libwebp's `dwebp`: 30ms and 13ms, 25ms and 11ms with its SIMD).
- A YCbCr or NYCbCrA image with chroma subsampled vertically and its top edge above 0 at an odd row no longer makes JPEG encoding (or anything that re-cuts it into bands) fail with an internal error.
- From level 7, PNG output tries no filter against the filters the rows choose, every 32KB of rows, and keeps the smaller: screenshots, icons and drawings 10–19% smaller; photographs unchanged.
- PNG levels 8 and 9 search for the smallest file: rows are filtered as oxipng's Brute strategy filters them (every filter tried), and the data is compressed with a Go port of libdeflate's near-optimal parsing instead of `compress/flate`, its blocks split by cost and its symbols priced at their entropy, as zopfli does. On 17 screenshots, photographs and game artwork, 12.2% (8) and 13.5% (9) smaller than level 7, against 12.8% for oxipng `-o max` (in a sixth of its time) and 13.8% for ECT `-9`; the same file with every Go version. They take about 4MB (8) and 6.5MB (9) more memory per thread.
- The minimum Go version is 1.26 (0.1.0 required 1.27). PNG files written at levels 1 to 6 with Go 1.26 and Go 1.27 differ in their bytes but not in their pixels, as Go 1.27 rewrote `compress/flate`; at the default level, 7, and at 8 and 9 they are the same.
- Progressive JPEG chooses its scans as mozjpeg does (`optimize_scans`): files 0.4–14% smaller (2.4% on average over 18 images, 3.9% with `Trellis` and `QuantTable: QuantTableImageMagick`; with both, 0.7% smaller than mozjpeg's defaults at equal PSNR), with the same pixels. Single-threaded it takes 3–19% longer; the candidate scans are counted in parallel with `Threads`.
- `Animated()` no longer ignores an animation it cannot keep: an animated GIF, WebP or PNG to an output format other than GIF and WebP returns an error wrapping `errors.ErrUnsupported` instead of writing the first frame. A still image passes as before. So does `MaxFileSize` with `Animated()` on an animation, which was ignored.
- `Threads(n)` now extends to the number of CPUs (previously capped at 3). From 4 threads upwards, resizing, blurring, sharpening, modulation, rotation, and JPEG, PNG and lossy WebP encoding distribute their work across cores. Output is byte-for-byte identical for any number of threads.
- The default PNG compression level is now 7 (previously 6, the standard library's default; see above for how it is compressed): against Go 1.27's level 6, on 38 images, gradients, skies and icons 1.3 to 4.2 times smaller, screenshots 20–23% smaller, photographs 1.8–7.1% smaller, in 1.85 times the time on one core.
- PNG data is compressed in 256KB blocks (as in pigz), allowing multiple cores to share the work. At levels 1 to 6, when the image data fits within a single block, the file is identical to that of previous versions. With Go 1.27 or later, data of more than one block is compressed up to level 6 by `klauspost/compress`'s flate, which writes the bytes Go 1.27's `compress/flate` writes and takes each block's dictionary when it is reset, where `compress/flate`'s has to be made anew for every block: a 24-megapixel PNG allocates 3MB instead of 276MB and peaks at 9.2MB instead of 16.6MB (15.0MB instead of 23.0MB with 4 threads), 6% faster, with the same bytes.
- Animated GIF output: a single palette is chosen for all frames (`GIFOptions.Colors` and `NoDither` now apply to animations), and frames after the first store only the changed rectangle. `GIFOptions{FixedPalette: true}` continues to write full frames with the fixed palette, frame by frame. The pixel limit is checked before any frame is processed. `NoDither` also applies with `FixedPalette`.
- Lossy WebP: segment-based quantization, following libwebp's spatial noise shaping.
- Lossy WebP: `WebPOptions.Effort` applies to lossy output too (it was for lossless only). The default, 5, converts colours with sharp YUV, quantizes 4×4 blocks by trellis and chooses the loop filter levels by measuring, as libwebp does at its best settings; chroma DC errors are diffused at every effort; quantizers and rate-distortion weights were tuned on SSIMULACRA2 and butteraugli. On 14 images: 6.6% / 9.5% smaller than libwebp's default and 1.3% / 2.2% smaller than its best settings (`-m 6 -af -sharp_yuv`), at equal SSIMULACRA2 / butteraugli. Encoding takes 2 to 2.6 times as long; `Effort: 1` keeps the earlier speed and is still 6.1% / 4.8% smaller than libwebp's default.
- Lossy WebP with transparency: the alpha plane is coded by the lossless encoder at its default effort, through the better of no filter and the horizontal one (all four filters from effort 7), with the optimal parse for planes of up to 4 megapixels (all from effort 7), and the colours of transparent areas are made cheap, as libwebp does: a photograph in a soft round window 50% smaller (146KB to 73KB at quality 75; libwebp 88KB), alpha chunks up to 6 times smaller (Tux's 2% smaller than libwebp's). Up to 8MB more memory while the plane is coded; under a low memory limit the alpha chunk (not the pixels) can differ, as lossless files can.
- Lossy WebP is decoded to the pixels libwebp, and so browsers, show, to the byte: chroma interpolated between samples and libwebp's integer conversion (before, each chroma sample covered its 2×2 block and the colours differed by about half a level on average, up to 20–40 at colour edges). Decoded lossy WebP is now RGB; `Metadata()` still reports it as `ycbcr`.
- Lossless WebP chooses its LZ77 matches by the cheapest path through the pixels (the idea of libwebp’s TraceBackwards), searches further for repeats in screenshots, and tries several block sizes and numbers of Huffman groups: 5.5% smaller over 17 images at the default effort (screenshots 6–9%), in 3.8 times the time (42s against 11s on one core; libwebp's default takes 24s); 5.3% smaller in total than libwebp’s slowest setting (5.7% at effort 9, in 106s against its 308s). At every pixel the longest match at the nearest distances above and to the left is offered besides the hash chain's (a gradient mask 16% smaller), and prefix codes are evened out for run-length coding of their lengths when that pays, as libwebp does. Images of up to 4 megapixels also race a variant with libwebp's cross-color transform (icons and interface pictures with colour gradients 2–9% smaller), their split into Huffman groups is also tried without the colour cache (an icon 4% smaller), and their variants without the predictor also try matches only at distance 1 and a row up, as libwebp's "RLE" references (icons of few colours 3–4% smaller). A 6000×4000 photograph from a JPEG, whose noise leaves the parse little to find, comes out the same size, in 5.1s instead of 4.1s and at most 25.6MB instead of 22.2MB.
- Lossless WebP selects each band's predictors using the cost model as it stands at the start of that band, so that they can be selected in parallel; output files differ slightly.
- The command-line code has moved from `cmd/goimgtools` to package `cli`; `cmd/goimgtools` builds the same program.

### Fixed

- Lossy WebP at effort 5 and above (the default): the frame's loop filter level was written as that of the first segment, which the measuring often sets to 0 (the segment of the most textured macroblocks); decoders (libwebp, libvpx, this module's) then filter nothing in the whole frame, whatever the other segments' levels. It is now the highest of the segments' levels, which are written as they are. Smooth photographs at low sizes lost their loop filter: on one such photograph and 19 edited versions of it at quality 75, SSIMULACRA2 69.3 → 73.6 and butteraugli 1.28 → 1.19, the files the same size.
- `Extend` and `FitContain` of an image with transparency kept as NRGBA (a PNG with alpha) went through premultiplied 8-bit RGBA, which moved the colours of translucent pixels (by up to 255/alpha levels: 4 at alpha 55); they keep them now.
- `FastShrinkOnLoad` on a JPEG whose components are sampled in ways the decoder does not reduce itself (Go's test files 121121, 211211, 221122, 222112) failed with "shrink: unsupported pixel type"; the decoded image is averaged again.

- A compressed TIFF of a large image was one strip, which libvips (and so sharp) refuses once its compressed data exceeds 50MB: compressed images of more than 1MB of pixel data are now written in strips of about 1MB.
- On processors that fuse a multiplication and an addition into one instruction (ARM64, such as Apple silicon and AWS Graviton, POWER, s390x, RISC-V, LoongArch), some results could differ from those on x86 by a level or a pixel: `Linear`, the blend modes, `Convolve` with fractional weights, the palettes of GIF and few-colour PNG, the Mitchell kernel, watermarks placed by ratio, the size of `Rotate`, `Blur`'s boxes, text outlines, and choices made by the lossless WebP and jpegli encoders. Every product is now rounded before it is added, as on x86, and CI checks that the compiler fuses none of them on those processors.
- A panic in an operation could leave the chain's input open, retaining memory budget and temporary files.
- `Frames().LoopCount` of a GIF was its repeat count, one less than the number of times it plays, which is what the field documents.

## 0.1.0 (2026-11-14)
